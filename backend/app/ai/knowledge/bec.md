# Business Email Compromise (BEC)

Business email compromise is fraud in which an attacker poses as an executive, colleague or supplier to trick staff into sending money or sensitive data. BEC messages often contain no links or attachments, so they can slip past technical filters.

Common signs: an urgent payment request, a change of supplier bank details, a request for gift cards, a request to keep the matter confidential, or a Reply-To address that differs from the sender.

Defence: verify any payment change or unusual request through a second channel, such as a phone call to a number you already have on file. Never use contact details from the suspicious email itself.

Finance teams should require dual approval for new payees and bank-detail changes. A short delay to verify is always acceptable. Real executives understand the policy.

If you think you sent money to a fraudster, contact your finance team and bank immediately. Fast action sometimes allows a transfer to be recalled.
